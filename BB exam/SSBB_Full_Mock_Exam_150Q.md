## Define Phase (23 Questions)

**Q1.** The primary purpose of the Define phase is to:

- A. Collect baseline process data
- **B. Identify the problem, scope, goals, and team ✓**
- C. Determine root causes of defects
- D. Implement process improvements

> **Answer: B** — Define establishes what problem is being solved, who is affected, what success looks like, and who is on the team. Data collection begins in Measure.

---

---

**Q2.** A project charter documents all of the following EXCEPT:

- A. Problem statement
- B. Goal statement
- C. Project scope
- **D. Root cause analysis ✓**

> **Answer: D** — Root cause analysis belongs to the Analyze phase. The charter covers problem, goal, scope, timeline, team, and business case.

---

---

**Q3.** VOC stands for:

- A. Value of Capability
- **B. Voice of the Customer ✓**
- C. Verification of Control
- D. Volume of Complaints

> **Answer: B** — VOC captures customer needs, expectations, and pain points. It is the starting point for defining what matters most.

---

---

**Q4.** CTQ stands for:

- A. Cost to Quality
- B. Control to Quantity
- **C. Critical to Quality ✓**
- D. Customer to Quantity

> **Answer: C** — CTQs are the measurable characteristics of a product or service that must meet customer requirements. They translate VOC into specific metrics.

---

---

**Q5.** A SIPOC diagram stands for:

- **A. Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Customers ✓**
- B. Systems, Inputs, Products, Outputs, Controls
- C. Suppliers, Improvement, Process, Outputs, Controls
- D. Standards, Inputs, Process, Objectives, Customers

> **Answer: A** — SIPOC provides a high-level macro view of the process and is one of the first tools used in the Define phase.

---

---

**Q6.** The business case in a project charter describes:

- A. The statistical tools that will be used
- **B. Why the project is important to the organization financially or strategically ✓**
- C. Who the process operators are
- D. The root cause of the problem

> **Answer: B** — The business case links the project to financial impact, strategic priorities, or customer satisfaction to justify resource investment.

---

---

**Q7.** A CTQ tree is used to:

- A. Map the process flow from start to finish
- **B. Break down high-level VOC needs into specific, measurable requirements ✓**
- C. Prioritize potential root causes
- D. Document lessons learned

> **Answer: B** — The CTQ tree translates broad customer needs (e.g., "fast delivery") into driver requirements and then into measurable CTQs (e.g., "delivery within 2 days, 98% on time").

---

---

**Q8.** Kano analysis categorizes customer requirements into three types. Which category describes features customers expect as a baseline and complain about only when absent?

- A. Delighters (Exciters)
- **B. Must-be requirements (Basic needs) ✓**
- C. Performance requirements (One-dimensional)
- D. Indifferent requirements

> **Answer: B** — Must-be (Basic) needs are assumed by customers — their absence causes dissatisfaction but their presence does not increase satisfaction. Examples: a hotel room having a bed, a car starting reliably.

---

---

**Q9.** In a RACI matrix, "A" (Accountable) means:

- A. The person who does the work
- **B. The single owner who is ultimately answerable for the outcome ✓**
- C. A person who provides input or expertise
- D. A person who is notified of the outcome

> **Answer: B** — Accountable = the one person who owns the decision or deliverable. Responsible = does the work. Consulted = provides input. Informed = kept in the loop.

---

---

**Q10.** Which stakeholder analysis tool maps stakeholders by their level of influence and interest in the project?

- A. SIPOC
- B. CTQ tree
- **C. Stakeholder analysis matrix (Power/Interest grid) ✓**
- D. Affinity diagram

> **Answer: C** — A Power/Interest grid helps teams prioritize stakeholder engagement: high power/high interest = manage closely; high power/low interest = keep satisfied, etc.

---

---

**Q11.** The "as-is" process map created during Define is used to:

- A. Document the improved future-state process
- **B. Understand the current process and identify obvious waste or complexity ✓**
- C. Set statistical control limits
- D. Validate the measurement system

> **Answer: B** — The as-is (current state) map reveals how the process actually works (not how it is supposed to work), often exposing rework loops, handoffs, and non-value-added steps.

---

---

**Q12.** A project with a scope that is too broad will likely result in:

- A. Faster project completion
- B. Better root cause identification
- **C. Resource drain, delays, and inconclusive results ✓**
- D. Higher process sigma

> **Answer: C** — Overly broad scope leads to teams chasing too many variables. Focused scope drives actionable analysis and faster results.

---

---

**Q13.** Which method is commonly used to collect VOC through structured customer interviews and surveys?

- A. FMEA
- B. Control chart
- **C. Focus groups, surveys, and interviews ✓**
- D. Gauge R&R

> **Answer: C** — VOC is gathered through direct methods (interviews, focus groups, complaint data, warranty data, surveys) and indirect methods (market research, social media, field reports).

---

---

**Q14.** The term "Y = f(X)" in the Define phase means:

- **B. The output (Y) is a function of input variables (Xs) that the team will identify ✓**
- A. The process yield equals the defect function
- C. The team should focus only on the output metric
- D. X and Y are axis labels on a control chart

> **Answer: B** — Y = f(X) is the fundamental Six Sigma equation: the output (defect, cycle time, cost) is driven by inputs. Define identifies Y; later phases find and optimize the Xs.

---

---

**Q15.** An affinity diagram is used to:

- A. Map supplier relationships
- **B. Organize large amounts of qualitative data (ideas, issues) into natural groupings ✓**
- C. Calculate process sigma
- D. Define control limits

> **Answer: B** — Affinity diagrams (also called KJ diagrams) group brainstormed ideas into themes, useful for organizing VOC feedback or potential problem areas.

---

---

**Q16.** When building a CTQ tree, which sequence is correct?

- A. CTQ → Driver → Need
- **B. Need → Driver → CTQ ✓**
- C. Driver → Need → CTQ
- D. CTQ → Need → Driver

> **Answer: B** — The CTQ tree flows from broad customer Need (e.g., "reliable car") → quality Driver (e.g., "engine performance") → measurable CTQ (e.g., "cold-start failure rate < 0.1%").

---

---

**Q17.** Which of the following is the BEST example of a well-written problem statement?

- A. "Our process is broken because operators make errors."
- B. "We need to implement a new ERP system to fix quality."
- **C. "Defect rate on Line 3 has averaged 4.2% since January, versus a target of 1%, costing $120K/month in rework. ✓"**
- D. "Customer complaints are too high and must be reduced."

> **Answer: C** — A strong problem statement is factual, quantified, time-bounded, and financially grounded. It states what is wrong without implying cause or solution.

---

---

**Q18.** A Black Belt is selecting between two projects. Project A has a financial benefit of $500K and sigma improvement from 3.0 to 4.5. Project B has a benefit of $200K and sigma improvement from 4.0 to 5.0. Using the project charter business case logic, which project is prioritized first?

- **A. Project A ✓**
- B. Project B
- C. Both are equal priority
- D. Neither without more data

> **Answer: A** — Project A delivers 2.5× the financial impact. Business case prioritization in Six Sigma typically weights financial benefit, strategic alignment, and customer impact. A larger absolute gain at a lower sigma baseline often has higher ROI.

---

---

**Q19.** Which element of the project charter is MOST likely to be revised after the Measure phase?

- A. Team composition
- B. Business case
- **C. Goal statement baseline and target ✓**
- D. Project sponsor

> **Answer: C** — The Measure phase establishes the actual baseline performance (which may differ from the assumed baseline in Define). Goal targets are often refined once real data is collected.

---

---

**Q20.** The "entitlement" of a process refers to:

- A. The current average performance
- **B. The best the process has ever achieved under optimal conditions ✓**
- C. The target set by the customer
- D. The performance at Six Sigma quality level

> **Answer: B** — Entitlement = the best sustained performance the process has demonstrated. It sets a realistic upper bound for improvement without capital investment, guiding goal-setting.

---

---

**Q21.** A project has a primary metric Y and two constraint metrics. The constraint metrics ensure that:

- **A. Improving Y does not negatively impact other important process outputs ✓**
- B. The project stays within budget
- C. The team does not exceed its timeline
- D. The measurement system is validated

> **Answer: A** — Constraint (or secondary) metrics act as guardrails. For example, reducing cycle time (Y) should not increase defect rate (constraint). This prevents sub-optimization.

---

---

**Q22.** The "transfer function" concept Y = f(X₁, X₂, … Xₙ) at the Define stage is used to:

- A. Calculate the process sigma
- **B. Frame the project hypothesis that output Y is determined by a set of key inputs Xs ✓**
- C. Set upper and lower control limits
- D. Validate the measurement system

> **Answer: B** — In Define, Y = f(X) is a conceptual frame. Later phases identify which Xs are significant (Analyze) and optimize them (Improve). Define focuses on clearly defining Y.

---

---

**Q23.** A Kano "delighter" (exciter) feature is best described as:

- A. A requirement that linearly increases satisfaction as performance improves
- B. A basic requirement customers assume will be present
- **C. An unexpected feature that causes disproportionately high customer delight ✓**
- D. A feature that causes dissatisfaction if absent but no satisfaction if present

> **Answer: C** — Delighters are unexpected, innovative features (e.g., a hotel leaving chocolates on the pillow). They are not expected, so their absence does not cause dissatisfaction, but their presence drives loyalty.

## Measure Phase (30 Questions)

**Q24.** Which type of data is counted (e.g., number of defects)?

- A. Continuous data
- **B. Attribute (discrete) data ✓**
- C. Ordinal data
- D. Ratio data

> **Answer: B** — Attribute/discrete data counts occurrences (defects, pass/fail). Continuous data is measured on a scale (temperature, weight).

---

---

**Q25.** The Mean of a dataset is best described as:

- A. The most frequently occurring value
- B. The middle value when data is sorted
- **C. The arithmetic average of all values ✓**
- D. The difference between the largest and smallest values

> **Answer: C** — Mean = sum ÷ count. Median = middle value; Mode = most frequent; Range = max − min.

---

---

**Q26.** What does "defect opportunity" mean in Six Sigma?

- A. A chance to improve the process
- **B. Any point in the process where a defect can occur ✓**
- C. The total number of defects found
- D. A failed inspection step

> **Answer: B** — DPMO = (Defects ÷ (Units × Opportunities)) × 1,000,000.

---

---

**Q27.** A histogram is primarily used to:

- A. Track defects over time
- **B. Display the frequency distribution of a dataset ✓**
- C. Compare two process means
- D. Map the relationship between two variables

> **Answer: B** — Histograms show shape, center, and spread of data distribution.

---

---

**Q28.** Which measure of central tendency is MOST resistant to outliers?

- A. Mean
- B. Mode
- **C. Median ✓**
- D. Range

> **Answer: C** — The median (middle value) is not pulled toward extreme values the way the mean is.

---

---

**Q29.** Standard deviation is a measure of:

- A. Central tendency
- **B. Process spread (variation) ✓**
- C. Process centering
- D. Defect frequency

> **Answer: B** — Standard deviation (σ) quantifies spread around the mean. Smaller σ = less variation.

---

---

**Q30.** Which chart displays the cumulative percentage of defects by category?

- A. Histogram
- B. Box plot
- **C. Pareto chart ✓**
- D. Run chart

> **Answer: C** — Pareto uses the 80/20 principle to identify the vital few categories driving most defects.

---

---

**Q31.** A process is "in statistical control" when:

- A. All output meets specification limits
- **B. Only common-cause variation is present ✓**
- C. Cpk > 1.33
- D. No defects are produced

> **Answer: B** — Statistical control ≠ capable. A process can be in control yet produce defects if centered poorly or too variable.

---

---

**Q32.** DPMO = 3,400 corresponds to approximately which sigma level (with 1.5σ shift)?

- A. 3.0σ
- B. 4.0σ
- **C. 4.5σ ✓**
- D. 5.0σ

> **Answer: C** — 3,400 DPMO ≈ 4.5σ (long-term). The 6σ benchmark is 3.4 DPMO; 4σ ≈ 6,210 DPMO.

---

---

**Q33.** In a Gauge R&R study, %GRR (Study Variation) < 10% means:

- A. Unacceptable — the gauge must be replaced
- **B. Acceptable measurement system ✓**
- C. Marginal — acceptable only for non-critical characteristics
- D. The gauge needs calibration only

> **Answer: B** — <10% = acceptable; 10–30% = marginal; >30% = unacceptable.

---

---

**Q34.** Which sampling method divides the population into subgroups and samples from each proportionally?

- A. Simple random
- **B. Stratified ✓**
- C. Cluster
- D. Systematic

> **Answer: B** — Stratified sampling ensures each subgroup is represented, reducing sampling error.

---

---

**Q35.** A box plot displays which five-number summary?

- A. Mean, mode, median, range, variance
- **B. Min, Q1, median, Q3, max ✓**
- C. Mean, ±1σ, ±2σ, ±3σ
- D. Min, mean, mode, max, range

> **Answer: B** — Whiskers extend to 1.5 × IQR; points beyond are flagged as potential outliers.

---

---

**Q36.** If tolerance = 20 units and σ = 4, what is Cp?

- **A. 0.83 ✓**
- B. 1.00
- C. 1.33
- D. 0.67

> **Answer: A** — Cp = Tolerance ÷ (6σ) = 20 ÷ 24 ≈ 0.83. Cp < 1.00 means spread exceeds specification width.

---

---

**Q37.** Repeatability in Gauge R&R measures:

- A. Variation between appraisers
- **B. Variation when the same appraiser measures the same part repeatedly ✓**
- C. Variation between gauges
- D. Overall measurement accuracy

> **Answer: B** — Repeatability = Equipment Variation (EV). Reproducibility = Appraiser Variation (AV).

---

---

**Q38.** A run chart is most useful for:

- A. Comparing variation to specification limits
- **B. Identifying trends and patterns over time ✓**
- C. Calculating process sigma
- D. Determining measurement system adequacy

> **Answer: B** — Run charts show time-ordered data without control limits, detecting trends, shifts, and cycles.

---

---

**Q39.** Which distribution models the number of defects per unit when events are rare and independent?

- A. Normal
- B. Binomial
- **C. Poisson ✓**
- D. Exponential

> **Answer: C** — Poisson models count of rare independent events per unit at a constant average rate.

---

---

**Q40.** Precision refers to:

- A. Closeness to true value
- **B. Consistency of repeated measurements ✓**
- C. Ability to detect small differences
- D. Range of values the gauge can measure

> **Answer: B** — Precision = repeatability. Accuracy = closeness to truth. Precise but inaccurate = systematically biased.

---

---

**Q41.** When the process mean equals the midpoint of spec limits, Cp and Cpk are:

- A. Cp > Cpk
- **B. Cp = Cpk ✓**
- C. Cpk > Cp
- D. Both equal zero

> **Answer: B** — Cpk ≤ Cp always. Equal only when the process is perfectly centered.

---

---

**Q42.** Which control chart monitors the proportion of defective items?

- A. X-bar & R
- B. I-MR
- **C. p-chart ✓**
- D. c-chart

> **Answer: C** — p-chart = proportion defective. c-chart = count of defects (constant unit size). u-chart = defects per unit (variable size).

---

---

**Q43.** The Central Limit Theorem states that:

- A. All manufacturing processes follow a normal distribution
- **B. Sample means from any population approach normality as n increases ✓**
- C. Process variation decreases as n increases
- D. Median equals mean for symmetric distributions

> **Answer: B** — The CLT underpins X-bar chart construction; typically n ≥ 30 is sufficient for the approximation.

---

---

**Q44.** A process has USL = 50, LSL = 30, μ = 43, σ = 2.5. What is Cpk?

- **A. 0.93 ✓**
- B. 1.07
- C. 1.33
- D. 0.80

> **Answer: A** — Cpk = min[(50−43)/7.5, (43−30)/7.5] = min[0.93, 1.73] = **0.93**.

---

---

**Q45.** A process produces 45 defects in 500 units with 6 opportunities. What is the DPMO?

- **A. 15,000 ✓**
- B. 90,000
- C. 45,000
- D. 9,000

> **Answer: A** — DPMO = [45 ÷ (500 × 6)] × 1,000,000 = **15,000** ≈ 3.67σ.

---

---

**Q46.** For Binomial with n = 20, p = 0.05: mean and variance are:

- **A. Mean = 1.0, Variance = 0.95 ✓**
- B. Mean = 0.05, Variance = 0.95
- C. Mean = 1.0, Variance = 1.0
- D. Mean = 4.0, Variance = 0.95

> **Answer: A** — Mean = np = 1.0; Variance = np(1−p) = 0.95.

---

**Q47.** Sample size to detect a 1.5σ shift with 90% power, α = 0.05 (two-tailed):

- A. n ≈ 19
- B. n ≈ 30
- **C. n ≈ 44 ✓**
- D. n ≈ 52

> **Answer: C** — n = [(z_α/2 + z_β)/δ]² = [(1.96+1.28)/1.5]² ≈ 44. Always round up.

**Q48.** In ANOVA Gauge R&R, the Appraiser × Part interaction is tested first because:

- A. It is always significant
- **B. If non-significant (p > 0.25), it is pooled into error to improve main effect estimates ✓**
- C. It measures repeatability directly
- D. It is required by the AIAG MSA manual for all studies

> **Answer: B** — Pooling non-significant interactions reduces degrees of freedom in error and improves F-test sensitivity for Appraiser and Part effects.

---

---

**Q49.** If repeatability variance = 4 and reproducibility variance = 9, total GRR σ is:

- **A. 3.61 ✓**
- B. 13.0
- C. 6.5
- D. 2.65

> **Answer: A** — Total GRR σ = √(4+9) = √13 ≈ **3.61**. Variances add; SDs do not.

---

---

**Q50.** A p-chart with p̄ = 0.04, n = 100. UCL is approximately:

- **A. 0.099 ✓**
- B. 0.079
- C. 0.116
- D. 0.060

> **Answer: A** — UCL = 0.04 + 3√[0.04×0.96/100] = 0.04 + 0.059 ≈ **0.099**.

---

---

**Q51.** Gauge resolution should be at least:

- **A. 1/10th of the process variation or specification tolerance ✓**
- B. 1/2 of the specification tolerance
- C. Equal to the standard deviation
- D. Greater than the control limit range

> **Answer: A** — The 10-to-1 rule: resolution ≤ 1/10 of tolerance. Poor resolution inflates apparent repeatability and limits ndc.

---

---

**Q52.** A process has σ_total² = 25, σ_part² = 16. GRR σ is:

- A. 41
- B. 16
- **C. 3 ✓**
- D. 5

> **Answer: C** — σ_GRR² = 25 − 16 = 9; σ_GRR = √9 = **3**. %GRR = (3/5) × 100 = 60% — unacceptable.

**Q53.** An I-MR chart is used when:

- A. Subgroup size n = 5
- **B. Only one measurement is taken per time period (n = 1) ✓**
- C. Data is attribute (pass/fail)
- D. Subgroup size varies

> **Answer: B** — I-MR uses the moving range between consecutive individual values to estimate short-term σ.

## Analyze Phase (30 Questions)

**Q54.** The primary goal of the Analyze phase is to:

- A. Implement solutions
- B. Collect baseline data
- **C. Identify and verify the root causes of the problem ✓**
- D. Set up control charts

> **Answer: C** — Analyze uses statistical and graphical tools to move from symptoms (Y) to confirmed root causes (vital few Xs).

---

---

**Q55.** A fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram is used to:

- A. Plot defect frequency over time
- **B. Visually organize potential causes of a problem ✓**
- C. Calculate process capability
- D. Map suppliers to outputs

> **Answer: B** — Fishbone diagrams structure brainstorming around cause categories (6 Ms), linking potential causes to the effect (problem).

---

---

**Q56.** The 6 Ms in a fishbone diagram stand for:

- **A. Man, Machine, Material, Method, Measurement, Mother Nature ✓**
- B. Motive, Means, Metrics, Management, Mission, Money
- C. Manufacturing, Marketing, Management, Metrics, Material, Mission
- D. Man, Money, Method, Machine, Measurement, Market

> **Answer: A** — These are the standard cause categories for manufacturing. Service processes sometimes use 4 Ps or 5 Ss variants.

---

---

**Q57.** A "5 Whys" analysis is used to:

- A. Identify all possible causes in one step
- **B. Drill down to the root cause by repeatedly asking "Why?" ✓**
- C. Prioritize defects by frequency
- D. Map the process flow

> **Answer: B** — Each "Why?" answer becomes the subject of the next "Why?" — driving from symptom to systemic root cause, typically in 3–7 iterations.

---

---

**Q58.** A scatter plot is used to:

- A. Show the distribution of one variable
- **B. Show the relationship between two continuous variables ✓**
- C. Identify the most frequent defect category
- D. Track defects over time

> **Answer: B** — Scatter plots display correlation (positive, negative, or none) between X and Y variables.

---

---

**Q59.** A p-value of 0.03 with α = 0.05 means:

- A. H₀ is true with 3% probability
- **B. Reject H₀ — the result is statistically significant ✓**
- C. The alternative hypothesis is false
- D. Sample size was insufficient

> **Answer: B** — p < α → reject H₀. p-value = probability of observing the data (or more extreme) if H₀ were true.

---

---

**Q60.** Regression analysis primarily models:

- A. Whether two population variances are equal
- **B. The relationship between one or more inputs (X) and an output (Y) ✓**
- C. Whether data is normally distributed
- D. The number of defects per unit

> **Answer: B** — Simple linear regression: Y = β₀ + β₁X + ε. Multiple regression adds more Xs.

---

---

**Q61.** A Pareto chart is used in Analyze to:

- A. Show process flow
- B. Plot data over time
- **C. Identify the vital few causes contributing to the majority of defects ✓**
- D. Measure process capability

> **Answer: C** — In Analyze, Pareto charts prioritize which X factors or defect types deserve the most investigation attention.

---

---

**Q62.** A multi-vari chart identifies which three sources of variation?

- **A. Positional (within piece), cyclical (piece-to-piece), temporal (time-to-time) ✓**
- B. Repeatability, reproducibility, part-to-part
- C. Short-term, long-term, measurement
- D. Common cause, special cause, assignable cause

> **Answer: A** — Multi-vari charts help focus DOE resources by visually revealing which variation family is dominant before statistical testing.

---

---

**Q63.** An independent samples t-test is used when:

- A. Comparing more than two means
- **B. Comparing the means of two independent groups ✓**
- C. Comparing one sample mean to a target
- D. Comparing two related (paired) samples

> **Answer: B** — Two-sample t-test: H₀: μ₁ = μ₂. Paired t-test is used when measurements are matched (before/after on the same unit).

---

---

**Q64.** ANOVA (Analysis of Variance) is used to:

- A. Compare two population variances
- **B. Compare means of three or more groups simultaneously ✓**
- C. Test whether data is normally distributed
- D. Calculate process capability

> **Answer: B** — One-way ANOVA tests H₀: μ₁ = μ₂ = … = μk. Significant result tells you at least one mean differs; post-hoc tests identify which pairs.

---

---

**Q65.** R² (coefficient of determination) in regression represents:

- A. The strength of the negative correlation
- **B. The proportion of Y variation explained by the model ✓**
- C. The slope of the regression line
- D. The residual error

> **Answer: B** — R² = 0.80 means 80% of the variation in Y is explained by the X variable(s) in the model.

---

---

**Q66.** Which test compares two population variances?

- A. t-test
- B. ANOVA
- **C. F-test (Levene's or Bartlett's test) ✓**
- D. Chi-square goodness-of-fit

> **Answer: C** — The F-test (or Levene's for non-normal data) tests H₀: σ₁² = σ₂². Equal variance is an assumption of the 2-sample t-test.

---

---

**Q67.** A chi-square test of independence is used for:

- A. Comparing two continuous means
- **B. Testing whether two categorical variables are related ✓**
- C. Assessing normality
- D. Comparing three or more variances

> **Answer: B** — Chi-square independence test: H₀ = the two categorical variables are independent. Uses a contingency table of observed vs. expected frequencies.

---

---

**Q68.** In hypothesis testing, "failing to reject H₀" means:

- A. H₀ is proven true
- **B. There is insufficient evidence to conclude a difference exists ✓**
- C. The alternative hypothesis is confirmed
- D. The test should be repeated with a different α

> **Answer: B** — Not rejecting H₀ is not the same as proving it true. It simply means the data do not provide enough evidence against it at the chosen α level.

---

---

**Q69.** A process capability analysis shows Cp = 1.50 and Cpk = 0.90. This indicates:

- A. The process is capable and centered
- **B. The process has adequate spread but is significantly off-center ✓**
- C. The process spread is too wide
- D. The process is both capable and centered

> **Answer: B** — Cp > Cpk indicates off-centering. Large gap (1.50 vs 0.90) = the process mean is substantially shifted toward one specification limit.

---

---

**Q70.** A Mann-Whitney U test is used as a non-parametric alternative to:

- A. One-way ANOVA
- **B. Two-sample t-test ✓**
- C. Paired t-test
- D. Chi-square test

> **Answer: B** — Mann-Whitney U compares medians (or rank distributions) of two independent groups when normality cannot be assumed.

---

---

- **B. Make the chart behave more like a standard Shewhart chart (sensitive to large shifts) ✓**
- C. Eliminate the need for control limits

- B. A measurement system error
