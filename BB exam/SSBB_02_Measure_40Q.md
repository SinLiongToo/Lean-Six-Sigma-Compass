# Six Sigma Black Belt — Measure Phase · 40 Questions with Answers

> Difficulty mix: 10 Easy · 16 Medium · 14 Hard

---

## Easy (Q1–Q10)

**Q1.** Which type of data is counted (e.g., number of defects)?

- A. Continuous data
- **B. Attribute (discrete) data ✓**
- C. Ordinal data
- D. Ratio data

> **Answer: B** — Attribute/discrete data counts occurrences (defects, pass/fail). Continuous data is measured on a scale (temperature, weight).

---

**Q2.** The Mean of a dataset is best described as:

- A. The most frequently occurring value
- B. The middle value when data is sorted
- **C. The arithmetic average of all values ✓**
- D. The difference between the largest and smallest values

> **Answer: C** — Mean = sum ÷ count. Median = middle value; Mode = most frequent; Range = max − min.

---

**Q3.** What does "defect opportunity" mean in Six Sigma?

- A. A chance to improve the process
- **B. Any point in the process where a defect can occur ✓**
- C. The total number of defects found
- D. A failed inspection step

> **Answer: B** — DPMO = (Defects ÷ (Units × Opportunities)) × 1,000,000.

---

**Q4.** A histogram is primarily used to:

- A. Track defects over time
- **B. Display the frequency distribution of a dataset ✓**
- C. Compare two process means
- D. Map the relationship between two variables

> **Answer: B** — Histograms show shape, center, and spread of data distribution.

---

**Q5.** Which measure of central tendency is MOST resistant to outliers?

- A. Mean
- B. Mode
- **C. Median ✓**
- D. Range

> **Answer: C** — The median (middle value) is not pulled toward extreme values the way the mean is.

---

**Q6.** Standard deviation is a measure of:

- A. Central tendency
- **B. Process spread (variation) ✓**
- C. Process centering
- D. Defect frequency

> **Answer: B** — Standard deviation (σ) quantifies spread around the mean. Smaller σ = less variation.

---

**Q7.** Which chart displays the cumulative percentage of defects by category?

- A. Histogram
- B. Box plot
- **C. Pareto chart ✓**
- D. Run chart

> **Answer: C** — Pareto uses the 80/20 principle to identify the vital few categories driving most defects.

---

**Q8.** A process is "in statistical control" when:

- A. All output meets specification limits
- **B. Only common-cause variation is present ✓**
- C. Cpk > 1.33
- D. No defects are produced

> **Answer: B** — Statistical control ≠ capable. A process can be in control yet produce defects if centered poorly or too variable.

---

**Q9.** A Gauge R&R study is conducted to assess:

- A. Process capability vs. specification limits
- **B. The proportion of total variation attributable to the measurement system ✓**
- C. Whether data follows a normal distribution
- D. Number of defects per unit

> **Answer: B** — Gauge R&R partitions variation into part-to-part vs. measurement system (repeatability + reproducibility).

---

**Q10.** Which sampling technique selects every Nth item?

- A. Stratified sampling
- B. Cluster sampling
- **C. Systematic sampling ✓**
- D. Convenience sampling

> **Answer: C** — Systematic sampling provides even time coverage without a full population list.

## Medium (Q11–Q26)

**Q11.** DPMO = 3,400 corresponds to approximately which sigma level (with 1.5σ shift)?

- A. 3.0σ
- B. 4.0σ
- **C. 4.5σ ✓**
- D. 5.0σ

> **Answer: C** — 3,400 DPMO ≈ 4.5σ (long-term). The 6σ benchmark is 3.4 DPMO; 4σ ≈ 6,210 DPMO.

---

**Q12.** In a Gauge R&R study, %GRR (Study Variation) < 10% means:

- A. Unacceptable — the gauge must be replaced
- **B. Acceptable measurement system ✓**
- C. Marginal — acceptable only for non-critical characteristics
- D. The gauge needs calibration only

> **Answer: B** — <10% = acceptable; 10–30% = marginal; >30% = unacceptable.

---

**Q13.** Which sampling method divides the population into subgroups and samples from each proportionally?

- A. Simple random
- **B. Stratified ✓**
- C. Cluster
- D. Systematic

> **Answer: B** — Stratified sampling ensures each subgroup is represented, reducing sampling error.

---

**Q14.** A box plot displays which five-number summary?

- A. Mean, mode, median, range, variance
- **B. Min, Q1, median, Q3, max ✓**
- C. Mean, ±1σ, ±2σ, ±3σ
- D. Min, mean, mode, max, range

> **Answer: B** — Whiskers extend to 1.5 × IQR; points beyond are flagged as potential outliers.

---

**Q15.** If tolerance = 20 units and σ = 4, what is Cp?

- **A. 0.83 ✓**
- B. 1.00
- C. 1.33
- D. 0.67

> **Answer: A** — Cp = Tolerance ÷ (6σ) = 20 ÷ 24 ≈ 0.83. Cp < 1.00 means spread exceeds specification width.

---

**Q16.** Repeatability in Gauge R&R measures:

- A. Variation between appraisers
- **B. Variation when the same appraiser measures the same part repeatedly ✓**
- C. Variation between gauges
- D. Overall measurement accuracy

> **Answer: B** — Repeatability = Equipment Variation (EV). Reproducibility = Appraiser Variation (AV).

---

**Q17.** A run chart is most useful for:

- A. Comparing variation to specification limits
- **B. Identifying trends and patterns over time ✓**
- C. Calculating process sigma
- D. Determining measurement system adequacy

> **Answer: B** — Run charts show time-ordered data without control limits, detecting trends, shifts, and cycles.

---

**Q18.** Which distribution models the number of defects per unit when events are rare and independent?

- A. Normal
- B. Binomial
- **C. Poisson ✓**
- D. Exponential

> **Answer: C** — Poisson models count of rare independent events per unit at a constant average rate.

---

**Q19.** Precision refers to:

- A. Closeness to true value
- **B. Consistency of repeated measurements ✓**
- C. Ability to detect small differences
- D. Range of values the gauge can measure

> **Answer: B** — Precision = repeatability. Accuracy = closeness to truth. Precise but inaccurate = systematically biased.

---

**Q20.** When the process mean equals the midpoint of spec limits, Cp and Cpk are:

- A. Cp > Cpk
- **B. Cp = Cpk ✓**
- C. Cpk > Cp
- D. Both equal zero

> **Answer: B** — Cpk ≤ Cp always. Equal only when the process is perfectly centered.

---

**Q21.** Which control chart monitors the proportion of defective items?

- A. X-bar & R
- B. I-MR
- **C. p-chart ✓**
- D. c-chart

> **Answer: C** — p-chart = proportion defective. c-chart = count of defects (constant unit size). u-chart = defects per unit (variable size).

---

**Q22.** The Central Limit Theorem states that:

- A. All manufacturing processes follow a normal distribution
- **B. Sample means from any population approach normality as n increases ✓**
- C. Process variation decreases as n increases
- D. Median equals mean for symmetric distributions

> **Answer: B** — The CLT underpins X-bar chart construction; typically n ≥ 30 is sufficient for the approximation.

---

**Q23.** Linearity in a measurement system refers to:

- A. Variation between appraisers
- **B. Consistency of bias across the gauge's operating range ✓**
- C. Ability to measure small differences
- D. Stability of measurements over time

> **Answer: B** — Poor linearity = bias changes from low to high measurement values.

---

**Q24.** A Type I error (α) means:

- A. Failing to detect a real difference
- **B. Rejecting H₀ when it is true (false positive) ✓**
- C. Using the wrong test
- D. Insufficient sample size

> **Answer: B** — Type I (α) = false alarm. Type II (β) = missed detection. Power = 1 − β.

---

**Q25.** Statistical power (1 − β) is the probability of:

- A. Making a Type I error
- **B. Correctly rejecting a false H₀ ✓**
- C. Accepting H₀ when true
- D. Choosing the wrong α level

> **Answer: B** — Power increases with larger n, larger effect size, or higher α.

---

**Q26.** The IQR (Interquartile Range) equals:

- A. Max − Min
- **B. Q3 − Q1 ✓**
- C. Mean − Median
- D. UCL − LCL

> **Answer: B** — IQR represents the middle 50% of data; robust against outliers.

## Hard (Q27–Q40)

**Q27.** A process has USL = 50, LSL = 30, μ = 43, σ = 2.5. What is Cpk?

- **A. 0.93 ✓**
- B. 1.07
- C. 1.33
- D. 0.80

> **Answer: A** — Cpk = min[(50−43)/7.5, (43−30)/7.5] = min[0.93, 1.73] = **0.93**.

---

**Q28.** A process produces 45 defects in 500 units with 6 opportunities. What is the DPMO?

- **A. 15,000 ✓**
- B. 90,000
- C. 45,000
- D. 9,000

> **Answer: A** — DPMO = [45 ÷ (500 × 6)] × 1,000,000 = **15,000** ≈ 3.67σ.

---

**Q29.** For Binomial with n = 20, p = 0.05: mean and variance are:

- **A. Mean = 1.0, Variance = 0.95 ✓**
- B. Mean = 0.05, Variance = 0.95
- C. Mean = 1.0, Variance = 1.0
- D. Mean = 4.0, Variance = 0.95

> **Answer: A** — Mean = np = 1.0; Variance = np(1−p) = 0.95.

---

**Q30.** Sample size to detect a 1.5σ shift with 90% power, α = 0.05 (two-tailed):

- A. n ≈ 19
- B. n ≈ 30
- **C. n ≈ 44 ✓**
- D. n ≈ 52

> **Answer: C** — n = [(z_α/2 + z_β)/δ]² = [(1.96+1.28)/1.5]² ≈ 44. Always round up.

---

**Q31.** In ANOVA Gauge R&R, the Appraiser × Part interaction is tested first because:

- A. It is always significant
- **B. If non-significant (p > 0.25), it is pooled into error to improve main effect estimates ✓**
- C. It measures repeatability directly
- D. It is required by the AIAG MSA manual for all studies

> **Answer: B** — Pooling non-significant interactions reduces degrees of freedom in error and improves F-test sensitivity for Appraiser and Part effects.

---

**Q32.** If repeatability variance = 4 and reproducibility variance = 9, total GRR σ is:

- **A. 3.61 ✓**
- B. 13.0
- C. 6.5
- D. 2.65

> **Answer: A** — Total GRR σ = √(4+9) = √13 ≈ **3.61**. Variances add; SDs do not.

---

**Q33.** A p-chart with p̄ = 0.04, n = 100. UCL is approximately:

- **A. 0.099 ✓**
- B. 0.079
- C. 0.116
- D. 0.060

> **Answer: A** — UCL = 0.04 + 3√[0.04×0.96/100] = 0.04 + 0.059 ≈ **0.099**.

---

**Q34.** Gauge resolution should be at least:

- **A. 1/10th of the process variation or specification tolerance ✓**
- B. 1/2 of the specification tolerance
- C. Equal to the standard deviation
- D. Greater than the control limit range

> **Answer: A** — The 10-to-1 rule: resolution ≤ 1/10 of tolerance. Poor resolution inflates apparent repeatability and limits ndc.

---

**Q35.** A process has σ_total² = 25, σ_part² = 16. GRR σ is:

- A. 41
- B. 16
- **C. 3 ✓**
- D. 5

> **Answer: C** — σ_GRR² = 25 − 16 = 9; σ_GRR = √9 = **3**. %GRR = (3/5) × 100 = 60% — unacceptable.

---

**Q36.** An I-MR chart is used when:

- A. Subgroup size n = 5
- **B. Only one measurement is taken per time period (n = 1) ✓**
- C. Data is attribute (pass/fail)
- D. Subgroup size varies

> **Answer: B** — I-MR uses the moving range between consecutive individual values to estimate short-term σ.

---

**Q37.** A control chart shows 9 consecutive points on one side of the center line. This indicates:

- A. Random common-cause variation
- **B. A special cause — process shift ✓**
- C. Measurement system error only
- D. No action needed as points are within limits

> **Answer: B** — Nelson/Western Electric run rules: 9 (or 8) consecutive points on one side = non-random shift requiring investigation.

---

**Q38.** % Bias = (0.8 ÷ 20) × 100 = ?

- **A. 4% ✓**
- B. 6%
- C. 8%
- D. 10%

> **Answer: A** — % Bias = |Bias| ÷ Tolerance × 100 = **4%**. Acceptable if < 5% of tolerance.

---

**Q39.** The relationship between Cp and sigma level (centered, no shift) is:

- **A. Sigma level = 3 × Cp ✓**
- B. Sigma level = Cp ÷ 3
- C. Sigma level = 6 × Cp
- D. Sigma level = Cp + 1.5

> **Answer: A** — Cp = (USL−LSL)/(6σ), so σ-level = 3 × Cp. Long-term = 3×Cp − 1.5 (with 1.5σ shift).

---

**Q40.** The Weibull distribution is used in reliability because:

- A. It only models constant failure rates
- **B. Its shape parameter β can model decreasing, constant, or increasing failure rates ✓**
- C. It is equivalent to the normal distribution for large samples
- D. It models attribute data only

> **Answer: B** — β < 1: infant mortality; β = 1: constant rate (exponential); β > 1: wear-out failures.
