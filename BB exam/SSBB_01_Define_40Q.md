# Six Sigma Black Belt — Define Phase · 40 Questions with Answers

> Difficulty mix: 10 Easy · 16 Medium · 14 Hard

---

## Easy (Q1–Q10)

**Q1.** The primary purpose of the Define phase is to:

- A. Collect baseline process data
- **B. Identify the problem, scope, goals, and team ✓**
- C. Determine root causes of defects
- D. Implement process improvements

> **Answer: B** — Define establishes what problem is being solved, who is affected, what success looks like, and who is on the team. Data collection begins in Measure.

---

**Q2.** A project charter documents all of the following EXCEPT:

- A. Problem statement
- B. Goal statement
- C. Project scope
- **D. Root cause analysis ✓**

> **Answer: D** — Root cause analysis belongs to the Analyze phase. The charter covers problem, goal, scope, timeline, team, and business case.

---

**Q3.** VOC stands for:

- A. Value of Capability
- **B. Voice of the Customer ✓**
- C. Verification of Control
- D. Volume of Complaints

> **Answer: B** — VOC captures customer needs, expectations, and pain points. It is the starting point for defining what matters most.

---

**Q4.** CTQ stands for:

- A. Cost to Quality
- B. Control to Quantity
- **C. Critical to Quality ✓**
- D. Customer to Quantity

> **Answer: C** — CTQs are the measurable characteristics of a product or service that must meet customer requirements. They translate VOC into specific metrics.

---

**Q5.** A SIPOC diagram stands for:

- **A. Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Customers ✓**
- B. Systems, Inputs, Products, Outputs, Controls
- C. Suppliers, Improvement, Process, Outputs, Controls
- D. Standards, Inputs, Process, Objectives, Customers

> **Answer: A** — SIPOC provides a high-level macro view of the process and is one of the first tools used in the Define phase.

---

**Q6.** The business case in a project charter describes:

- A. The statistical tools that will be used
- **B. Why the project is important to the organization financially or strategically ✓**
- C. Who the process operators are
- D. The root cause of the problem

> **Answer: B** — The business case links the project to financial impact, strategic priorities, or customer satisfaction to justify resource investment.

---

**Q7.** Which role is responsible for removing organizational barriers for a Six Sigma project?

- A. Black Belt
- B. Green Belt
- **C. Champion / Sponsor ✓**
- D. Process Owner

> **Answer: C** — The Champion/Sponsor is typically a senior leader who provides resources, removes roadblocks, and ensures alignment with business strategy.

---

**Q8.** The problem statement in a project charter should:

- A. Include the suspected root cause
- B. Propose a solution
- **C. Describe what is wrong, where, when, and how much ✓**
- D. List all improvement options

> **Answer: C** — A good problem statement is factual and specific: what is happening, where it occurs, when it started, and the magnitude. It does NOT include causes or solutions.

---

**Q9.** A goal statement should be:

- A. Vague to allow flexibility
- **B. Specific, measurable, and time-bound ✓**
- C. Focused on identifying root causes
- D. Written after root causes are confirmed

> **Answer: B** — Goal statements follow SMART criteria: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Example: "Reduce defect rate from 5% to 1% by Q4."

---

**Q10.** Process scope in a project charter defines:

- A. The statistical significance level
- B. The number of team members needed
- **C. Where the process starts and ends for this project ✓**
- D. The financial budget for improvements

> **Answer: C** — Scope prevents "scope creep" by explicitly stating the start and end boundaries of the process being improved.

---

## Medium (Q11–Q26)

**Q11.** A CTQ tree is used to:

- A. Map the process flow from start to finish
- **B. Break down high-level VOC needs into specific, measurable requirements ✓**
- C. Prioritize potential root causes
- D. Document lessons learned

> **Answer: B** — The CTQ tree translates broad customer needs (e.g., "fast delivery") into driver requirements and then into measurable CTQs (e.g., "delivery within 2 days, 98% on time").

---

**Q12.** Kano analysis categorizes customer requirements into three types. Which category describes features customers expect as a baseline and complain about only when absent?

- A. Delighters (Exciters)
- **B. Must-be requirements (Basic needs) ✓**
- C. Performance requirements (One-dimensional)
- D. Indifferent requirements

> **Answer: B** — Must-be (Basic) needs are assumed by customers — their absence causes dissatisfaction but their presence does not increase satisfaction. Examples: a hotel room having a bed, a car starting reliably.

---

**Q13.** In a RACI matrix, "A" (Accountable) means:

- A. The person who does the work
- **B. The single owner who is ultimately answerable for the outcome ✓**
- C. A person who provides input or expertise
- D. A person who is notified of the outcome

> **Answer: B** — Accountable = the one person who owns the decision or deliverable. Responsible = does the work. Consulted = provides input. Informed = kept in the loop.

---

**Q14.** Which stakeholder analysis tool maps stakeholders by their level of influence and interest in the project?

- A. SIPOC
- B. CTQ tree
- **C. Stakeholder analysis matrix (Power/Interest grid) ✓**
- D. Affinity diagram

> **Answer: C** — A Power/Interest grid helps teams prioritize stakeholder engagement: high power/high interest = manage closely; high power/low interest = keep satisfied, etc.

---

**Q15.** The "as-is" process map created during Define is used to:

- A. Document the improved future-state process
- **B. Understand the current process and identify obvious waste or complexity ✓**
- C. Set statistical control limits
- D. Validate the measurement system

> **Answer: B** — The as-is (current state) map reveals how the process actually works (not how it is supposed to work), often exposing rework loops, handoffs, and non-value-added steps.

---

**Q16.** A project with a scope that is too broad will likely result in:

- A. Faster project completion
- B. Better root cause identification
- **C. Resource drain, delays, and inconclusive results ✓**
- D. Higher process sigma

> **Answer: C** — Overly broad scope leads to teams chasing too many variables. Focused scope drives actionable analysis and faster results.

---

**Q17.** Which method is commonly used to collect VOC through structured customer interviews and surveys?

- A. FMEA
- B. Control chart
- **C. Focus groups, surveys, and interviews ✓**
- D. Gauge R&R

> **Answer: C** — VOC is gathered through direct methods (interviews, focus groups, complaint data, warranty data, surveys) and indirect methods (market research, social media, field reports).

---

**Q18.** The term "Y = f(X)" in the Define phase means:

- **B. The output (Y) is a function of input variables (Xs) that the team will identify ✓**
- A. The process yield equals the defect function
- C. The team should focus only on the output metric
- D. X and Y are axis labels on a control chart

> **Answer: B** — Y = f(X) is the fundamental Six Sigma equation: the output (defect, cycle time, cost) is driven by inputs. Define identifies Y; later phases find and optimize the Xs.

---

**Q19.** An affinity diagram is used to:

- A. Map supplier relationships
- **B. Organize large amounts of qualitative data (ideas, issues) into natural groupings ✓**
- C. Calculate process sigma
- D. Define control limits

> **Answer: B** — Affinity diagrams (also called KJ diagrams) group brainstormed ideas into themes, useful for organizing VOC feedback or potential problem areas.

---

**Q20.** The tollgate review at the end of the Define phase verifies that:

- A. Root causes have been confirmed
- B. Improvements have been piloted
- **C. The problem, scope, goal, and team are clearly defined and approved ✓**
- D. Control charts are in place

> **Answer: C** — Define tollgate checks: Is the business case solid? Is the problem clear? Is scope appropriate? Is the team resourced? Are metrics identified? Only then does the project proceed to Measure.

---

**Q21.** Cost of Poor Quality (COPQ) includes which categories?

- A. Prevention costs only
- B. Appraisal costs only
- **C. Internal failure costs and external failure costs ✓**
- D. All four: prevention, appraisal, internal failure, external failure

> **Answer: D** — COPQ (Cost of Poor Quality) includes all four PAF categories: Prevention, Appraisal, Internal Failure, External Failure. COPQ is often specifically the failure costs, but the full quality cost model includes all four.

---

**Q22.** A swimlane process map adds which dimension compared to a basic flowchart?

- **A. Who (which person, team, or system) performs each step ✓**
- B. Statistical variation at each step
- C. Cycle time and cost at each step
- D. Defect rates at each step

> **Answer: A** — Swimlane maps organize process steps into horizontal or vertical lanes by role/department, making handoffs and accountability immediately visible.

---

**Q23.** The project timeline in a project charter is typically displayed using a:

- A. Control chart
- B. SIPOC diagram
- **C. Gantt chart or milestone plan ✓**
- D. Pareto chart

> **Answer: C** — A Gantt chart shows tasks, durations, dependencies, and milestones across time — standard for Six Sigma project planning within the charter.

---

**Q24.** Which statement best describes "scope creep"?

- A. Adding more team members than planned
- **B. Uncontrolled expansion of project boundaries beyond the original charter ✓**
- C. Reducing the goal target mid-project
- D. Changing the measurement metric

> **Answer: B** — Scope creep occurs when additional requirements or process areas are added without corresponding adjustment to schedule, resources, or budget — a leading cause of project failure.

---

**Q25.** The primary output of the Define phase is:

- A. A validated measurement system
- B. Identified root causes
- **C. An approved project charter with defined scope and goals ✓**
- D. A list of implemented solutions

> **Answer: C** — Define delivers: project charter, SIPOC, VOC/CTQ, as-is process map, and stakeholder analysis — all approved through the tollgate.

---

**Q26.** In Six Sigma, a "defect" is defined as:

- A. Any unit produced by the process
- B. A step that takes longer than planned
- **C. Any output that fails to meet a customer CTQ requirement ✓**
- D. A measurement below the process average

> **Answer: C** — A defect is any nonconformance with a specification or customer requirement. This definition drives DPMO calculations and sigma level.

---

## Hard (Q27–Q40)

**Q27.** When building a CTQ tree, which sequence is correct?

- A. CTQ → Driver → Need
- **B. Need → Driver → CTQ ✓**
- C. Driver → Need → CTQ
- D. CTQ → Need → Driver

> **Answer: B** — The CTQ tree flows from broad customer Need (e.g., "reliable car") → quality Driver (e.g., "engine performance") → measurable CTQ (e.g., "cold-start failure rate < 0.1%").

---

**Q28.** Which of the following is the BEST example of a well-written problem statement?

- A. "Our process is broken because operators make errors."
- B. "We need to implement a new ERP system to fix quality."
- **C. "Defect rate on Line 3 has averaged 4.2% since January, versus a target of 1%, costing $120K/month in rework. ✓"**
- D. "Customer complaints are too high and must be reduced."

> **Answer: C** — A strong problem statement is factual, quantified, time-bounded, and financially grounded. It states what is wrong without implying cause or solution.

---

**Q29.** A Black Belt is selecting between two projects. Project A has a financial benefit of $500K and sigma improvement from 3.0 to 4.5. Project B has a benefit of $200K and sigma improvement from 4.0 to 5.0. Using the project charter business case logic, which project is prioritized first?

- **A. Project A ✓**
- B. Project B
- C. Both are equal priority
- D. Neither without more data

> **Answer: A** — Project A delivers 2.5× the financial impact. Business case prioritization in Six Sigma typically weights financial benefit, strategic alignment, and customer impact. A larger absolute gain at a lower sigma baseline often has higher ROI.

---

**Q30.** Which element of the project charter is MOST likely to be revised after the Measure phase?

- A. Team composition
- B. Business case
- **C. Goal statement baseline and target ✓**
- D. Project sponsor

> **Answer: C** — The Measure phase establishes the actual baseline performance (which may differ from the assumed baseline in Define). Goal targets are often refined once real data is collected.

---

**Q31.** The "entitlement" of a process refers to:

- A. The current average performance
- **B. The best the process has ever achieved under optimal conditions ✓**
- C. The target set by the customer
- D. The performance at Six Sigma quality level

> **Answer: B** — Entitlement = the best sustained performance the process has demonstrated. It sets a realistic upper bound for improvement without capital investment, guiding goal-setting.

---

**Q32.** A project has a primary metric Y and two constraint metrics. The constraint metrics ensure that:

- **A. Improving Y does not negatively impact other important process outputs ✓**
- B. The project stays within budget
- C. The team does not exceed its timeline
- D. The measurement system is validated

> **Answer: A** — Constraint (or secondary) metrics act as guardrails. For example, reducing cycle time (Y) should not increase defect rate (constraint). This prevents sub-optimization.

---

**Q33.** The "transfer function" concept Y = f(X₁, X₂, … Xₙ) at the Define stage is used to:

- A. Calculate the process sigma
- **B. Frame the project hypothesis that output Y is determined by a set of key inputs Xs ✓**
- C. Set upper and lower control limits
- D. Validate the measurement system

> **Answer: B** — In Define, Y = f(X) is a conceptual frame. Later phases identify which Xs are significant (Analyze) and optimize them (Improve). Define focuses on clearly defining Y.

---

**Q34.** A Kano "delighter" (exciter) feature is best described as:

- A. A requirement that linearly increases satisfaction as performance improves
- B. A basic requirement customers assume will be present
- **C. An unexpected feature that causes disproportionately high customer delight ✓**
- D. A feature that causes dissatisfaction if absent but no satisfaction if present

> **Answer: C** — Delighters are unexpected, innovative features (e.g., a hotel leaving chocolates on the pillow). They are not expected, so their absence does not cause dissatisfaction, but their presence drives loyalty.

---

**Q35.** The financial benefits of a Six Sigma project should ideally be validated by:

- A. The Black Belt alone
- B. The project sponsor
- **C. The finance department or a financial analyst ✓**
- D. The process operators

> **Answer: C** — Financial validation by finance ensures credibility, proper accounting treatment (hard vs. soft savings), and alignment with P&L statements. Black Belts estimate; finance confirms.

---

**Q36.** "Hard savings" in a Six Sigma project are best described as:

- **A. Tangible, verifiable reductions in cost that appear on the P&L (e.g., headcount reduction, material savings) ✓**
- B. Estimated cost avoidance from future defects
- C. Productivity improvements that do not reduce actual spending
- D. Customer satisfaction score improvements

> **Answer: A** — Hard savings directly reduce actual expenses (materials, labor, scrap). Soft savings (cost avoidance, productivity gains) are real but harder to verify on the income statement.

---

**Q37.** Which of the following best describes the relationship between VOC, CTQ, and the project Y metric?

- A. VOC and CTQ are the same thing; Y is derived from Xs
- **B. VOC → translated into CTQs → operational Y metric selected from CTQs ✓**
- C. Y is set by management, then CTQs and VOC are reverse-engineered
- D. CTQs replace VOC in the Measure phase

> **Answer: B** — The chain: raw customer voice (VOC) → structured quality requirements (CTQ) → measurable project output metric (Y). Each step adds specificity and measurability.

---

**Q38.** A project champion asks the Black Belt to expand the project scope to include three additional product lines. The BEST response is to:

- A. Immediately expand scope to satisfy the champion
- B. Refuse and escalate to the MBB
- **C. Assess impact on timeline and resources, update the charter if approved, and document the change ✓**
- D. Split the project without informing the champion

> **Answer: C** — Scope changes require formal charter revision, impact assessment, and stakeholder approval. Accepting scope changes without documentation leads to unmanaged scope creep.

---

**Q39.** In the Define phase, a process map is drawn at what level of detail?

- A. Task-level (every individual action documented)
- **B. Macro-level (major process blocks, 5–8 steps) suitable for SIPOC ✓**
- C. Sub-process level (all decision points and loops)
- D. Code-level (system workflow diagrams)

> **Answer: B** — In Define, SIPOC and high-level process maps show major steps only. Detailed process maps (with decision points, rework loops, cycle times) are developed in Measure and Analyze.

---

**Q40.** The DMAIC project selection criterion of "controllability" means:

- A. The team can statistically control all process variables
- **B. The solution is within the team's authority and sphere of influence to implement ✓**
- C. The process has existing SPC charts
- D. The project requires no capital investment

> **Answer: B** — A good Six Sigma project must be solvable by the team. If the root cause lies outside the team's influence (e.g., supplier contracts requiring executive approval), the project may stall without the right sponsorship.
