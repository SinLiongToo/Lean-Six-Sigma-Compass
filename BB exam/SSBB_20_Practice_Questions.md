# Six Sigma Black Belt — 20 Practice Questions

> Covers all five DMAIC phases · 4 questions per phase

---

## Define Phase

**Q1.** A project charter primarily serves to:

- A. Document the statistical analysis plan
- **B. Formally authorize the project and clarify scope, goals, and resources ✓**
- C. List all potential root causes of a defect
- D. Identify the optimal control limits

> **Explanation:** The project charter authorizes the project, defines scope, goals, timelines, and resources. It is the foundational Define-phase document.

---

**Q2.** Which tool is BEST suited to map a high-level process showing inputs, outputs, suppliers, and customers?

- A. FMEA
- **B. SIPOC diagram ✓**
- C. Control chart
- D. Affinity diagram

> **Explanation:** SIPOC (Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Customers) provides a macro-level view of the process and is a core Define-phase tool.

---

**Q3.** Voice of the Customer (VOC) is translated into measurable product requirements using:

- A. Control plans
- B. Gauge R&R
- **C. Critical to Quality (CTQ) tree ✓**
- D. Pareto chart

> **Explanation:** A CTQ tree breaks down VOC statements into specific, measurable quality characteristics the process must deliver.

---

**Q4.** A RACI matrix is used during the Define phase to:

- A. Rank defects by frequency
- **B. Clarify roles and responsibilities for team members ✓**
- C. Map causes and effects
- D. Set specification limits

> **Explanation:** RACI (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) prevents confusion about who owns each task or decision.

---

## Measure Phase

**Q5.** Process sigma level is calculated using DPMO. If DPMO = 6,210, what is the approximate sigma level?

- A. 3.0
- **B. 4.0 ✓**
- C. 4.5
- D. 5.0

> **Explanation:** DPMO of 6,210 corresponds to approximately 4-sigma performance (with the standard 1.5σ shift applied).

---

**Q6.** A Gauge R&R study is conducted to assess:

- A. Process capability versus specification limits
- **B. The proportion of total variation attributable to the measurement system ✓**
- C. Whether data follows a normal distribution
- D. The number of defects per unit

> **Explanation:** Gauge R&R partitions total observed variation into part-to-part variation vs. measurement system variation (repeatability + reproducibility).

---

**Q7.** Which sampling technique ensures every Nth item in production is selected?

- A. Stratified sampling
- B. Cluster sampling
- **C. Systematic sampling ✓**
- D. Convenience sampling

> **Explanation:** Systematic sampling selects every kth unit, providing even coverage over time without requiring a complete list upfront.

---

**Q8.** A Cp = 1.33 indicates:

- A. The process is centered and produces ~63 DPMO
- **B. The process spread uses 75% of the tolerance, meeting the 4-sigma minimum standard ✓**
- C. The process is off-center but capable
- D. The process spread equals the specification width exactly

> **Explanation:** Cp = 1.33 means the process spread (6σ) fits the tolerance with a 4σ margin on each side — a common industry minimum for capable processes.

---

## Analyze Phase

**Q9.** In a fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram, the 6 M categories are:

- **A. Man, Machine, Material, Method, Measurement, Mother Nature ✓**
- B. Motive, Means, Metrics, Management, Mission, Money
- C. Manufacturing, Marketing, Management, Metrics, Material, Mission
- D. Man, Money, Method, Machine, Measurement, Market

> **Explanation:** The classic 6 Ms are Man (people), Machine, Material, Method, Measurement, and Mother Nature (environment).

---

**Q10.** A p-value of 0.03 in a hypothesis test (α = 0.05) means:

- A. There is a 3% chance the null hypothesis is true
- **B. The null hypothesis is rejected; the result is statistically significant ✓**
- C. The alternative hypothesis is false
- D. The sample size was insufficient

> **Explanation:** p < α (0.03 < 0.05) means we reject H₀. The p-value is the probability of observing results as extreme as the data if H₀ were true — not the probability H₀ is true.

---

**Q11.** Regression analysis is used in Six Sigma primarily to:

- A. Determine whether a process is in statistical control
- **B. Quantify the relationship between input variables (Xs) and an output (Y) ✓**
- C. Prioritize defects by frequency and cumulative impact
- D. Test whether two population proportions are equal

> **Explanation:** Regression models Y = f(X) to understand and quantify how input variables drive output performance.

---

**Q12.** Multi-vari charts are used to identify:

- A. Optimal factor settings in a DOE
- **B. Sources of variation: positional, cyclical, and temporal ✓**
- C. Whether the measurement system is adequate
- D. Upper and lower control limits

> **Explanation:** Multi-vari charts visually display three sources of variation — within a piece, piece-to-piece, and time-to-time — helping focus analysis before detailed DOE.

---

## Improve Phase

**Q13.** In a 2² full factorial DOE with two factors (A, B), how many treatment combinations are run?

- A. 2
- **B. 4 ✓**
- C. 8
- D. 16

> **Explanation:** 2² = 4 treatment combinations: (−,−), (+,−), (−,+), and (+,+).

---

**Q14.** A response surface methodology (RSM) study is MOST useful when:

- A. You need to screen many factors quickly
- **B. You want to model curvature and find the optimal operating region ✓**
- C. You are verifying a control plan
- D. You are testing whether two means are equal

> **Explanation:** RSM (e.g., Central Composite Design) adds center and axial points to model quadratic effects and locate the process optimum.

---

**Q15.** Poka-yoke devices are implemented during the Improve phase primarily to:

- A. Detect outliers in control charts
- **B. Prevent defects or make errors immediately obvious ✓**
- C. Calculate process sigma
- D. Perform hypothesis testing on process means

> **Explanation:** Poka-yoke (mistake-proofing) either prevents an error from occurring or detects it immediately, reducing reliance on inspection.

---

**Q16.** When prioritizing improvement solutions, which tool maps solutions by effort versus impact?

- A. FMEA
- B. Control plan
- **C. Effort–impact matrix (prioritization matrix) ✓**
- D. Gauge R&R

> **Explanation:** An effort–impact matrix plots solutions on a 2×2 grid of effort vs. benefit, enabling teams to select quick wins and high-value projects rationally.

---

## Control Phase

**Q17.** An X-bar and R chart signals an out-of-control condition when:

- A. All points fall within 1 sigma of the center line
- **B. A point falls outside the 3-sigma control limits, or specific run rules are violated ✓**
- C. Cp > 1.33
- D. The measurement system Gauge R&R > 10%

> **Explanation:** Western Electric / Nelson rules define OOC signals: one point outside 3σ limits, 8 consecutive points on one side of the mean, 6 consecutive points trending, etc.

---

**Q18.** A control plan documents which of the following for each key process input/output variable?

- A. The DOE factor settings
- **B. The process step, characteristic, specification, measurement method, sample plan, and reaction plan ✓**
- C. The DPMO and sigma level only
- D. The organizational chart and budget

> **Explanation:** A control plan specifies what to measure, how to measure it, sample frequency and size, and the reaction plan if a variable goes out of spec.

---

**Q19.** Statistical Process Control (SPC) is most appropriate for:

- A. Designing a new product from scratch
- B. Identifying root causes of past defects
- **C. Monitoring an ongoing process to detect and signal changes in real time ✓**
- D. Calculating the cost of poor quality

> **Explanation:** SPC uses control charts to distinguish common-cause variation (inherent noise) from special-cause variation (signals requiring investigation).

---

**Q20.** A process with a Cpk = 0.85 is best described as:

- A. Highly capable; no action needed
- B. Marginally capable with Cpk meeting the 1.33 standard
- **C. Not capable; the process is producing defects beyond specification limits ✓**
- D. Off-center but within 6-sigma quality levels

> **Explanation:** Cpk < 1.00 indicates the process is producing output beyond at least one specification limit. The minimum acceptable Cpk is typically 1.33 (4σ) for established processes.
